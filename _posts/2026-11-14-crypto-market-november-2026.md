The crypto market is going through a sharp correction in mid-November 2026. Bitcoin has fallen below $70,000, Ethereum has moved toward $1,900, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) have seen substantial outflows. At the same time, US inflation and the prospect of higher interest rates are weighing on investors’ appetite for risk.

This article is a snapshot of information available on **November 14, 2026**. Prices and fund flows change quickly, and reported figures can vary by source and update time.

## Bitcoin and Ethereum: the correction deepens

On November 13, Bitcoin fell below $70,000 for the first time since August. CoinMarketCap’s historical data put its daily close at about **$68,249**. During trading on November 14, the price briefly fell below $68,000. Market reports put Bitcoin more than 20% below its late-October levels.

Ethereum followed the broader move, falling toward **$1,900**, while the total crypto market capitalization stood at roughly **$2.3 trillion** in data available on November 14. Treat these as reference points rather than fixed quotes: crypto markets trade around the clock, and data providers use different methodologies and observation times.

Selling accelerated as Bitcoin moved below levels watched by traders using borrowed funds. Automatic liquidations can magnify an initial move: falling prices trigger forced sales, which can push prices lower still. This helps explain some of the volatility, but it does not determine the market’s long-term direction on its own.

## ETF outflows weaken a source of institutional demand

Spot ETFs have given traditional investors a way to gain exposure to Bitcoin and Ethereum. During the November 9–13 trading week, US crypto ETFs tracked by Crypto.news recorded about **$1.53 billion in combined net outflows**. Bitcoin funds accounted for the largest share, followed by Ethereum funds.

These outflows do not prove that institutional investors have permanently abandoned digital assets. They do show that, during this period, ETF demand was not enough to offset selling pressure. Binance Research also reported that October ended with outflows from spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs as total crypto market capitalization fell 9.1% to about $2.70 trillion.

## Inflation and interest rates shape risk appetite

The macroeconomic backdrop remains challenging for volatile assets. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that the Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose **0.4% in October from September**, after a 0.6% increase in September. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3% month over month.

The figures came in above market expectations and added to concerns that the Federal Reserve could keep rates elevated or raise them. Higher rates can support the dollar and bonds, making non-yielding assets such as Bitcoin less attractive to some investors. This is one possible market relationship, not a complete explanation of crypto price moves; liquidity, positioning and sector-specific news also matter.

## Binance breach puts custody risks back in focus

On October 29, a security incident associated with Binance resulted in the reported theft of approximately **$6.3 billion** in digital assets. Chainalysis attributed the attack, with high confidence, to TraderTraitor, a group associated by authorities with North Korea. On November 12, the FBI said it had obtained authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the stolen funds. Authorities and private-sector partners had previously announced that they had frozen more than $2.7 billion combined.

The incident does not mean that every platform or network is equally vulnerable. It does highlight the risks of concentrated custody and the importance of operational controls, transaction monitoring and incident-response plans. For users, keeping assets on a platform and taking self-custody involve different security trade-offs and responsibilities.

## Regulatory uncertainty remains in the picture

In September, a US Senate procedural vote blocked a bill intended to establish a federal framework for crypto markets. The vote was 49 to 50, with disagreement over ethics provisions and officials’ financial interests preventing the bill from moving forward. Until clearer rules are adopted, companies and investors will need to follow both agency decisions and legislative developments.

Uncertainty can affect product launches, institutional access and how platforms operate. The practical impact depends on the jurisdiction and each company’s activities; developments in the US do not automatically describe conditions in the European Union or elsewhere.

## What to watch next

Several indicators may help clarify the market’s direction in the second half of November:

- **ETF flows:** A return to net inflows could point to stabilizing demand; persistent withdrawals would signal continued pressure.
- **Macroeconomic data and rate expectations:** Markets will watch inflation, bond yields and Federal Reserve communications.
- **Liquidations and volatility:** A reduction in leveraged positions could ease sharp swings, but would not guarantee a recovery.
- **The Binance response:** Fund recovery and the investigation’s findings will matter for confidence in trading and custody infrastructure.
- **Legislative progress:** Clearer rules could influence participation by companies and institutional investors.

## Conclusion: a defensive market, with no long-term verdict yet

As of November 14, 2026, the crypto market is facing falling prices, ETF outflows, liquidations and macroeconomic uncertainty. Together, these signals point to a period of risk aversion, but they are not enough on their own to confirm either a recovery or a prolonged downturn. The coming weeks will depend in part on whether demand returns and liquidity and macroeconomic pressures ease.
