Crypto markets remain under heavy pressure on **Sunday, November 15, 2026**. Bitcoin is trading around $64,500 in weekend market reports after briefly falling toward $62,000, while Ether has slipped to roughly $1,790. Selling has spread across major altcoins, and US spot Bitcoin ETFs have recorded seven consecutive trading sessions of net outflows.

Prices can move sharply between reporting times, especially over weekends when traditional markets are closed. The figures below are a snapshot of reports published on November 15, not fixed closing prices.

## Bitcoin rebounds from a three-month low

Bitcoin fell to just above **$62,000**, its lowest level in about three months, before recovering to around **$64,500** in weekend trading. It was down roughly **15% over the week** and more than **22% over the past month**, according to market data cited by Decrypt. CoinDesk also reported that Ether, XRP and Solana were each down about 17% over the week during the latest leg of the sell-off.

The rebound from the intraday low does not yet establish that the decline has ended. Rapid moves are common in crypto, and price readings vary by exchange, index and timestamp. The broader signal is that selling has reached beyond Bitcoin: the total crypto market value was reported near **$2.3 trillion**, down about 4.5% on the day in Decrypt’s snapshot.

## Ethereum and XRP join the downturn

Ether traded near **$1,790**, with the same report putting its daily decline at about 8.4%. XRP fell below **$1** and reached its lowest level since November 2024, according to CoinDesk. US-listed XRP exchange-traded funds also saw net outflows last week, their largest weekly withdrawal since March, adding another sign of reduced risk appetite across the sector.

Bitcoin’s market share tends to rise when investors sell smaller, more volatile tokens. That pattern can signal a flight toward relative liquidity within crypto, but it does not mean Bitcoin is insulated from losses or that altcoins are certain to keep underperforming.

## ETF outflows point to weaker demand

US spot Bitcoin ETFs shed approximately **$829 million** during the week, extending their outflow streak to seven consecutive trading days. November outflows had reached about **$2.1 billion** in Decrypt’s November 15 report. Ethereum ETFs lost around **$336 million** for the week, though they recorded a small net inflow on Friday that ended a six-day outflow streak. Solana ETFs lost about $28 million over the same week.

ETF flows are one measure of demand, not a complete account of every investor’s activity. Still, continued redemptions alongside falling prices suggest that these funds have not provided enough buying pressure to counter the current wave of selling. The next US trading sessions will show whether the outflow streak continues once ETF markets reopen.

## Macro pressure and leverage amplify volatility

Investors are also reassessing the outlook for US interest rates after inflation data came in hotter than expected. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that the Consumer Price Index rose **0.4% in October from September**, while core CPI increased 0.3% month over month. The figures have added to concern that the Federal Reserve may keep rates high or raise them, a backdrop that can weigh on demand for risk-sensitive assets.

Leveraged trading can make the moves more abrupt. When prices fall through levels that trigger margin calls, exchanges automatically close some positions, creating additional market orders. This can accelerate a decline without explaining all of its causes. Spot selling, fund flows, macroeconomic expectations and thin weekend liquidity may all contribute at the same time.

## What to watch as markets reopen

The next few days may offer a clearer read on whether the weekend sell-off is stabilizing or continuing. Key indicators include:

- **Bitcoin ETF flows:** renewed net inflows would suggest demand is returning; continued redemptions would extend the current pressure.
- **Bitcoin’s recovery from the weekend low:** a sustained move matters more than a short-lived bounce from around $62,000.
- **Ether and altcoin performance:** stabilization across major tokens would indicate that selling pressure is broadening less aggressively.
- **Interest-rate expectations:** new inflation and Federal Reserve signals can quickly change the appeal of risk assets.
- **Liquidations and trading depth:** easing forced sales and healthier order books could reduce the risk of sharp intraday swings.

## Conclusion: crypto remains in a risk-off phase

As of November 15, Bitcoin’s move toward $62,000, persistent ETF outflows and broad weakness in Ether and XRP point to a market still focused on reducing risk. The rebound toward $64,500 offers little confirmation by itself that a durable recovery has begun. Investors will be watching fund flows and price action after US markets reopen, while keeping in mind that short-term market readings are highly volatile.
